role: >-
  Joined a campus visit to IBM Taiwan, paired with an intro to their
  SkillsBuild learning platform. It was a career-oriented session rather than
  a deep technical one — a technologist on where IBM stands today, a
  career-journey talk, an HR perspective on how they evaluate candidates, and
  an office tour.
summary: >-
  What stayed with me wasn't any single talk, but a thread running through
  all of them: transformation — and not just the corporate kind. One slide
  showed how the skills enterprises look for have shifted: coding, ranked
  first a decade ago, now sits near the bottom of the list. Not because code
  stopped mattering, but because producing it is no longer the bottleneck.
  Another speaker, coming from a technical background, shared how she's now
  being asked to grow into a sales-facing role. Companies transform, and the
  people inside them are expected to transform too. I felt this personally.
  The lowered barrier to coding is exactly how someone like me — a finance
  student — could start building at all. But the same shift moves the real
  differentiator elsewhere: toward knowing which problems are worth solving,
  and the domain knowledge it takes to see them. Thanks to LiFUNd and the IBM
  Taiwan team for the visit.
